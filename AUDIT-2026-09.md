# OshiCart — Full System Audit

**Date:** 2 September 2026 · **Scope:** product, WhatsApp automation, business funnel, competitors, security and operations · **Method:** production database queries, code reading, browser click-throughs, and web research. Every number below was measured, not estimated.

---

## 0. The six things that matter

1. **The WhatsApp flaw you noticed is structural, not a typo.** Every customer message is sent from OshiCart's single number and tells the customer *"the seller will confirm"* or *"contact the store"* — but a customer who replies to that message reaches OshiCart's unattended number, and the reply is forwarded to **you**, never to the merchant. The messages invite a conversation the store cannot have. Details and fix in §1.
2. **The business problem is activation, not features.** 37 real merchants; 14 never added a product; 26 have never received an order; 25 are suspended; **3 pay** (MRR N$2,397). 45 orders ever, 7 in the last 30 days. Every roadmap decision should be judged against that funnel (§2).
3. **A third of customer order confirmations never arrive.** `order_placed` failed 14 of 41 times. Some are test orders, some are numbers with no WhatsApp — and the merchant is never told the customer is unreachable (§1.3).
4. **No direct competitor exists in Namibia.** The nearest are SA's Shopstar (R220–740 + ~2.9% gateway) and Singapore's Take App (US$50/mo). Food delivery apps here take 15–30%. OshiCart's *zero commission, paid direct to merchant* is a genuine, defensible position — but the competitors all have two things OshiCart lacks: **the merchant's own WhatsApp number**, and **two-way chat** (§3).
5. **Nobody is watching production.** No error monitoring, analytics events go to stdout and vanish, 0 unit tests. The product-page crash last week ran for a day because nothing could have told anyone (§5).
6. **A Meta access token sat in a database log field for six months.** Redacted today; write-time scrubbing shipped with this audit (§5.2).

## 1. The WhatsApp automation — what's wrong

### 1.1 The flaw: messages invite replies the store never receives

**How it's built.** One WhatsApp Business number, registered to OshiCart, sends *every* message on the platform — to customers, merchants and admins. The sender the customer sees is "OshiCart", not the shop they bought from.

**What the messages say.** The live customer templates:

| Template | Wording | What happens if the customer replies |
|---|---|---|
| `order_placed` | "…The **seller will confirm** your order shortly. Tap below to track…" | Reply lands on OshiCart's number |
| `order_confirmed` | "…confirmed and being prepared. We'll let you know when it's ready!" | Same |
| `order_ready` | "…is ready for pickup. Please collect at your earliest convenience…" | Same |
| `order_cancelled` | "…has been cancelled. **Please contact the store** if you have any questions." | Same — and there is no button at all |
| `booking_reminder` | "…tap below and **let the store know on WhatsApp**…" | The button opens the store's *web page*, not WhatsApp |

**Where the reply goes.** The webhook receives the customer's reply and forwards it to the **admin** phones via `inbound_message_alert` ("Reply to them directly on their number, not to this message"). The merchant is never notified. The code comment in the webhook says it plainly: *"every automated message invites people to 'reply here'"* — and until the forwarding existed, those replies went nowhere at all.

So the customer experience is: *"Your order is confirmed. The seller will contact you."* → customer replies *"What time can I collect?"* → silence from the store (who never saw it), while you get an alert you can only act on by texting a stranger about someone else's pizza.

**Why it matters commercially.** A customer's first instinct on WhatsApp is to reply. Every one of those replies is a support moment, a cross-sell, or a cancellation the merchant should own. Right now they leak to you. It also explains why the Eagles Pizza enquiry put so much weight on "works with *our* WhatsApp Business number" — that's what they assume they're buying.

**Fix — two horizons.**

*Now (days):* Route inbound replies to the **merchant**, not the admin. The webhook already knows the customer's number; look up their most recent order → merchant → forward to `merchant.whatsapp_number` with the customer's number in the message so the merchant can reply directly. Add a **"Chat with {store}" URL button** (`wa.me/<merchant>`) to `order_placed`, `order_ready` and `order_cancelled`, so the obvious action is correct. Change "contact the store" to "tap below to message {{store}}".

*Next (weeks):* **Per-merchant sender.** Meta allows multiple phone numbers on one WhatsApp Business Account, and Embedded Signup lets a merchant connect their own number in-dashboard. Messages then arrive *from the shop*, replies go *to the shop*, and the brand is the merchant's. This is exactly what Take App sells, and it's the single feature most likely to move the 3-paying number. It's a real project — template approval per number, webhook routing, a settings flow — but it's the right destination.

### 1.2 Delivery failures — 32% of all messages ever sent failed

383 messages sent since March; **124 failed**. Broken down, three distinct problems:

| Problem | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-stock storm** | 85 alerts to one merchant in 66 seconds on 26 May, until Meta rate-limited the pair (`131056`). Dedup key is `low_stock_alert:<product>:<qty>` — every sale at a new quantity is a "new" event, and the cron runs every 15 minutes. | **Open.** Key it per product per day and send one digest. |
| **Parameter mismatch** | `order_ready` failed 7 of 11 times (Mar–Apr) with `#132000` — code sent a different variable count than the approved Meta template. | Fixed in April. No guard exists to stop it recurring; `check-*` scripts cover code-vs-code drift but not code-vs-Meta. |
| **Unreachable customers** | 10 `order_placed` confirmations "undeliverable" — the number has no WhatsApp, or was mistyped. The number *is* normalised correctly before sending (verified), so these are genuine dead numbers. | **Open.** The merchant is never told. Show "customer not reachable on WhatsApp — call them" on the order card. |
| **Marketing experiment** | `abandoned_cart_reminder` is a *marketing*-category template. Meta withholds those from ~1% of users (`130472`) and it also failed on dead numbers: 6 of 9 failed, **0 of 14 abandoned checkouts recovered**. | Working as Meta intends. Recovery is a marketing message and needs consent; see §4.7. |

### 1.3 Two smaller things

- `order_ready`'s fourth variable is a whole sentence ("ready for pickup. Please collect at your earliest convenience") stitched into "…from {{3}} is {{4}}. Tap below…". It reads fine for pickup; check the delivery variant reads as a sentence too.
- `hello_world` test sends from March are still in the message log (6 rows, all failed). Cosmetic, but they skew failure rates in any dashboard you build.

## 2. Where the business actually stands

All figures from production, real merchants only (demo/practice stores excluded).

### 2.1 The funnel

| Stage | Merchants | % of signups |
|---|---|---|
| Signed up | **37** | 100% |
| Added ≥1 product | 23 | 62% |
| Received ≥1 order, ever | 11 | 30% |
| Received an order in the last 30 days | **4** | 11% |
| Paying subscription | **3** (all Oshi-Pro) | 8% |

Subscription status: 3 active · 9 on trial · **25 suspended** (15 hard, 7 soft, 3 lapsed Pro). Nine signups in the last 30 days — acquisition is working; conversion is not.

**MRR: N$2,397.** Total orders ever: 45 · last 30 days: 7 · average order N$1,280 (skewed by Octovia's B2B catalogue).

### 2.2 What customers actually do

- 52 customers · 1 review · 5 coupons created, 1 ever used
- **33 of 45 orders (73%) are cash on delivery/collection.** Namibia is still cash. Any feature that assumes digital payment is a feature for a quarter of orders.
- 6 delivery orders, 2 via courier. Pickup dominates.
- 11 of 45 orders cancelled (24%) — mostly the auto-cancel of unpaid orders. Worth checking how many were real customers who intended to pay.
- 14 abandoned checkouts, 0 recovered.

### 2.3 What this says

The platform's engineering is now ahead of its adoption. The 26 merchants who never got an order didn't leave because a feature was missing — most never got as far as needing one. The engagement cron's own comment says it: *"most stores that die, die here"* (no products). The highest-leverage work is not a new capability; it is getting a merchant from signup to first order, by hand if necessary, and learning what stops them.

## 3. Competitors — what they do, what we do

### 3.1 Namibia

There is **no Namibian WhatsApp-store-builder competitor** in operation. Tambula (COVID-era street-vendor portal) is gone; Shop-it Namibia is an Instagram page; Namshop.de exports crafts to Germany. Standard Bank's market note describes Namibian e-commerce as nascent, with most launches "short-lived due to outdated content and lack of secure payment methods" and consumers unwilling to enter card details online.

The real local competition is **food delivery**: Dial-A-Meal (Windhoek, Swakop, Walvis), Tap-A-Meal, Buddy Eats, Yango Deli. They take the Mr D model — 15–30% commission — and own the customer relationship. A restaurant on those apps is renting its customers.

**Implication:** OshiCart's *0% commission, money straight to the merchant, customer data is yours* is not a slogan, it's the actual gap in the market. The Eagles Pizza enquiry (delivery-only pizza, asking about commission, POS and driver integration) is the archetype customer, and food is the category where the local competition is strongest.

### 3.2 South Africa (the reference market — NAD≈ZAR)

| | OshiCart | Shopstar | Take App (SG, global) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry plan | Free (20 products / 20 orders) | R220 (25 products) | Free |
| Mid plan | N$399 (200 / 300) | R475 (100 products) | — |
| Top plan | N$799 unlimited | R740 unlimited | US$50 ≈ N$900 |
| Trial | 30 days | 14 days | — |
| Payment fee | **0%** — merchant paid direct | 2.85–2.95% gateway (Yoco/iKhokha) | Gateway fees apply |
| Card payments | Off (DPO built, disabled) | Yes | Yes |
| Own WhatsApp number | No | n/a | **Yes** |

**Reading it honestly:** on price OshiCart is competitive and on *total cost* it is the cheapest by a distance, because it charges nothing on the money. On Namibian payment rails and on selling services and hire, it is ahead of everyone. Where it is behind is precisely the WhatsApp layer it is named for — own number, inbox — plus the operational features a restaurant asks for first (zones, modifiers, cards).

### 3.3 The WhatsApp cost shift you need to know about

From **1 October 2026** Meta bills per message rather than per conversation. Rest-of-Africa rates: **utility ≈ US$0.004 (≈ N$0.07)**, **marketing ≈ US$0.0225 (≈ N$0.40)**. A typical order (placed, confirmed, ready, completed + merchant alert) costs ≈ N$0.35. At 800 orders/month that is ≈ N$280 — comfortably inside a N$799 plan. But a **broadcast** to 500 customers costs ≈ N$200 per send, so the broadcast feature needs a per-tier cap or a per-message price before a merchant discovers it on your bill.

## 5. Security, reliability and operations

### 5.1 What's solid

- Column-level grants on `merchants` verified again today — payment credentials (including the new `wayame_number`) are invisible to `anon`; public columns readable. RLS on orders/customers/subscriptions held in QA round 3.
- `place_order` runs the money maths and stock/booking locks in the database, not the browser.
- 21 regression check scripts, each proven to fail on the bug it guards. This is the reason recent fixes have stayed fixed.
- OTP and order-lookup code sends are throttled. Crons require `CRON_SECRET`. Security headers set.
- 0 production npm advisories after last week's upgrade.

### 5.2 What isn't

| Finding | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **No error monitoring** | High | No Sentry or equivalent. The product-page RSC crash ran for ~a day; the only signal was a merchant's complaint. Add Sentry (or Vercel's error tracking) with an alert to your WhatsApp. |
| **Analytics events are not stored** | High | `/api/analytics/event` writes to stdout and nothing else. Every `onboarding_step_completed`, `checkout_started` etc. is lost. You cannot answer "where do merchants drop out?" — which is the §2 question. Persist to a table or PostHog. |
| **Access token in the message log** | High → fixed | Two rows from 18 March stored a fetch error quoting the full `Authorization: Bearer EAA…` header. The token was short-lived and has expired, but it sat in `whatsapp_messages.error_message` for six months. **Redacted in production today; `scrubSecrets()` now strips tokens before any error string is written.** |
| **Unthrottled public endpoints** | Medium | `/api/reports` (store reports → moderation queue) has no auth, no dedupe, no rate limit — one script can bury the safety queue. `/api/check-email`, `/api/referral/validate` allow enumeration. Reviews are correctly gated by tracking token. |
| **No unit tests** | Medium | 0 unit tests; 7 Playwright specs; the check scripts carry the load. Money maths (`place_order`) has no automated test of totals across VAT-inclusive/exclusive, coupons and deposits. |
| **Low-stock cron can storm** | Medium | §1.2. Also worth an outbound rate guard in `sendWhatsAppTemplate` (max N per recipient per minute) so no future bug can hit Meta's pair limit. |
| **Code-vs-Meta template drift** | Medium | Nothing verifies that `bodyVariableCounts` in code matches the approved template in Meta. A script that fetches templates via the Graph API and diffs would have caught the `order_ready` failures on day one. |
| **Client-side signup side-effects** | Low | Welcome message, admin alert and signup notification are fire-and-forget `fetch`es from the browser after `router.push`. Now backstopped by the cron sweep; long-term, move merchant creation server-side. |
| **Backups / PITR** | Verify | Supabase Pro supports point-in-time recovery; confirm it is enabled and that a restore has been rehearsed once. |

## 6. Recommended plan

### Next 2 weeks — stop the leaks
1. Route customer replies to the merchant; add "Chat with {store}" buttons; reword "contact the store" (§1.1).
2. Per-product-per-day low-stock key + digest; outbound per-recipient guard.
3. "Customer unreachable on WhatsApp" flag on the order card.
4. Abandoned-cart consent checkbox at checkout.
5. Persist analytics events; add Sentry with a WhatsApp alert.
6. Rate-limit `/api/reports`, `/api/check-email`, `/api/referral/validate`.
7. Set `OSHICART_ADMIN_WHATSAPP_NUMBERS` so you finally get signup alerts (pending from last week).

### 30–60 days — convert the funnel
8. **Activation sprint:** personally onboard the 9 trial merchants and the 14 with no products. Record every blocker. Build only what three or more of them hit.
9. Scheduled order time for products; multi-select modifiers; delivery zones + driver assignment — in that order, because that's the order a food business needs them.
10. Template drift check script (Graph API vs code).
11. Send the IPN letter; enable WayaMe the day P2P goes live; decide on re-enabling DPO cards.

### 60–90 days — own the channel
12. Per-merchant WhatsApp number via Embedded Signup. This is the feature that turns "OshiCart sends messages about your shop" into "your shop messages its customers", and it is the one every competitor charges for.
13. Loyalty, custom domains, staff accounts — only once ≥10 merchants are paying and asking.
